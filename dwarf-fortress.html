Strike the Earth! Your First Fortress

A beginner's guide to Dwarf Fortress: Steam Edition (the 2022+ graphical version from Bay 12 Games & Kitfox Games, with the mouse-driven UI).

Written for Frank Brophy · October 11, 2026 · Checked against the Dwarf Fortress Wiki pages for the current release (v53.16, the August 2026 patch). Hotkeys are the Steam defaults; you can see or change them in Settings → Keybindings. Classic ASCII-era keys appear only as side notes.

The one rule
Losing is fun. That's the community motto, and the wiki's Quickstart guide says it too: expect to lose a few fortresses while you learn. Each collapse teaches you something. Save often, and don't get too attached to fort #1.
Fastest start
The built-in tutorial is very good. The wiki recommends playing through it at least once. It picks a site, dwarves, and supplies for you and then walks you through the interface. You can replay any part of it later with the ? button next to the minimap (source). Use this guide alongside it, or afterward.

1. World generation & picking an embark site

Make a gentle world

From the main menu choose Create new world. Every world is randomly generated, so there's no "default" map. For a first game, the Quickstart guide suggests:

SettingSuggested value
World map sizeMedium
History length100 years
Number of civilizationsMedium
Number of sitesMedium
Number of beastsLow
Natural savageryVery Low
Mineral occurrenceEverywhere

Let generation finish before you continue. The wiki warns that stopping history early can limit what materials you can get at embark and through trade. Then pick Fortress mode. You'll be offered the tutorial (it picks a site for you) or Skip tutorial (you pick).

What a good first site looks like

The world map tooltip only describes the tile under your mouse. Your embark area covers several tiles, though, and they can hold different biomes. Hover over every tile in the embark rectangle before you commit, so you don't miss a hidden aquifer or evil biome (Quickstart: Choosing a good site).

  • No aquifer, or at worst a light one. A heavy aquifer floods your digging and is a classic beginner killer.
  • Trees: Woodland or Heavily Forested (Sparse at minimum). Wood is the only normal material for beds, and you'll want it for barrels and bins too (Embark: Plant life).
  • Water: a river, stream, or brook.
  • Soil (clay, sand, loam, etc.), ideally deep. Soil lets you farm underground without irrigating first.
  • Surroundings: Serene, Calm, or Wilderness. Avoid Savage and Evil for now. Savage lands have giant animal men. Evil lands can have disease-carrying rain and undead, and some reanimate the dead (Embark: Surroundings).
  • Temperature: Warm or Temperate.
  • Metals: iron at least; flux stone is a bonus (iron plus flux lets you make steel later).
  • Neighbors: stay away from goblins and anyone at war with you. Necromancer towers don't show in the neighbor list, so look for their icons on the map. The embark confirmation should warn you if you're too close.
  • Your civilization should be on the same landmass with at least two dwarven sites. Otherwise migrants can dry up after the second winter (Embark).

Using the embark site finder

  1. With the map zoomed out (region view), click Find embark location at the bottom of the screen.
  2. Set your criteria (for example: aquifer = No, trees, river, Calm/Wilderness surroundings, soil) and run the search.
  3. When it finishes, press Esc to see the results. Green rectangles are full matches and yellow ones are partial matches. Click one to zoom in and see the actual candidate sites.
  4. Hover over every tile, then place the embark rectangle. The default is 4×4; you can resize it with the arrow buttons at the top left to cut out a bad biome.
  5. If nothing matches, loosen the criteria. "Calm" is rare, so try "Wilderness". A world with no good sites is also a perfectly good reason to generate a new one.

Left-click zooms in. Right-click zooms out, or closes a menu if one is open. Choose origin civilization (on the zoomed-out map) lists the dwarven civs and highlights their sites. Source: Quickstart: Embark.

Play Now! vs. Prepare for the journey carefully

After you pick a site you get two options. You also get difficulty settings on the right:

  • Play Now! gives you a reasonable default party and supply list. Use this for your first fort.
  • Prepare for the journey carefully lets you spend skill points and embark points yourself. The wiki notes it "makes little difference in the long run," especially before you know what you'd change (Embark: Prepare carefully).
Beginner setting
Set Enemies to Off for fort #1. That turns off sieges, megabeast attacks, werebeast attacks, and agitated wildlife. Hostile wildlife already living on the map and dangerous environments still apply. These settings can be changed after embark too, except the invasion caps (Embark: Difficulty).

You always start with seven dwarves. With Play Now! their roles look like this (Embark: Play Now!):

What's in the wagon (Play Now! defaults)

Highlights from the wiki's supply list (exact items vary with what your civilization can make):

  • Copper (or bronze) picks and battle axes. Picks dig; an axe lets a woodcutter chop.
  • An iron (or steel) anvil, a wheelbarrow, a stepladder.
  • About 60 units of alcohol in barrels, plus meat, fish, and plump helmets.
  • Seeds: bags of plump helmet, cave wheat, sweet pod, pig tail, dimple cup, and quarry bush.
  • Rope, buckets, bags, thread/cloth, splints and crutches, two dogs, two cats, and a few other animals.
Prepare carefully, later
Each dwarf gets 10 skill ranks to assign (max 5 in any one skill), and you spend embark points on items and animals. Skills don't cost points. Good first tweaks once you've played a fort: bring extra food and booze, or extra logs if your site has few trees. You can save a setup as a reusable profile (Embark).

3. First-day checklist

When the wagon arrives, pause first with Space. Look around: move with WASD, change z-level (depth) with the scroll wheel, and zoom with Ctrl+scroll. F1 recenters on the wagon. Find the water, the hills, and the trees before anyone picks up a pick (Quickstart: Getting your bearings).

You don't control dwarves directly
You give the fortress a to-do list: dig here, build that, store things there. Dwarves who are allowed to do that kind of work pick up the tasks. If nobody has the right labor (or a pick, for mining), the job just sits there, often with no warning.

The checklist

  • Dig into a hillside. Open the dig menu (pickaxe button at the bottom, M), choose Dig/Mine, and drag a rectangle. If there's a cliff or hill near the wagon, tunnel straight in. Make the entry corridor about 10–20 tiles long, and 3 tiles wide if you want trade wagons to reach a depot later. On flat ground, channel a 3×3 pit to make ramps, go down one level, and tunnel sideways. Channeling deeper than one level can strand dwarves in a pit (Quickstart: Strike the Earth).
  • Give a second dwarf a pick. In the labor menu (Y), select the Miners work detail and tick a second dwarf. Play Now! comes with picks to spare. See section 4.
  • Stairs down. Dig → Stairs (M then T). Click the top tile, scroll down a level or more, and click again. The game places the right down / up-down / up stairs for you.
  • Stockpiles (P). Make a large general stockpile indoors and use its custom settings to exclude corpses, refuse, stone, gems, and wood. Those would clog it fast. Make a separate wood pile near the entrance and a refuse pile outside, which keeps rotting things and their miasma out of the fort. Dwarves will start hauling everything off the wagon (Quickstart: Stockpiles, Stockpile).
  • Cut a few trees (tree-chop designation; L in the quickstart). Start with 3–5; one tree gives several logs. Too many and your woodcutter does nothing else. In a pinch you can deconstruct the wagon for 3 logs.
  • Carpenter's workshop → beds, barrels, bins. Build menu (B) → Workshops → Carpenter's workshop. Click the finished workshop → Add new task and queue beds (wood only), barrels (for booze), and later bins.
  • Meeting area. Your wagon is the first meeting area. Once you have a room underground, make a Meeting Area zone (Z → Meeting Area, drag, Accept) so idle dwarves wait inside.
  • Underground farm with plump helmets. Dig a room in a soil layer (5×5 is fine), then Build → Farm plot (the quickstart's sequence is B O F P) and place a 3×3 plot. Click the plot and choose plump helmets for every season. Each season has its own tab; skip one and that field sits idle for that season (Quickstart: Farming, Farming).
  • Still & brewing. Build → Workshops → Farming → Still (quickstart: B O F L), ideally near the farm. Click it → Add new task → Brew drink from plant. Each brew needs an empty barrel or rock pot (Quickstart: Brewing, Brewing).
  • Dining hall / tavern. Dig a decent room, place tables and chairs (made at the carpenter's or stoneworker's workshop), and zone it as a Dining Hall. To make it a tavern later, open the zone's location button and pick New inn/tavern. Dwarves strongly prefer eating at a table with a chair (Tavern).
  • Bedrooms. The easy start is a Dormitory zone with a few beds: it's simple and needs fewer beds. Individual bedrooms (a small room with a bed and a door, zoned as Bedroom) make dwarves happier. The quickstart suggests putting sleeping areas at least ~8 tiles from noisy mining and woodcutting (Quickstart: Bedrooms).
  • Grazing animals (from the wagon) need a Pen/Pasture zone on grass, roughly 10×10 to start, or they'll starve.
  • Turn off Hunters and Fisherdwarves for now (see section 4) so nobody wanders off alone.
Shortcuts are optional
Everything above can be done by clicking the buttons along the bottom of the screen. The hotkeys come from the wiki's v50+ quickstart. If one doesn't match your game, check Settings → Keybindings and trust that over this guide.

Classic ASCII note: in the pre-Steam versions (0.47 and earlier) everything was keyboard-only. Digging lived under d (designations) and zones under i. Old tutorials and videos that use those keys are describing the old interface.

Mining safety
Don't dig into water. When newly revealed tiles are "damp", the game cancels digging there, and that's your warning. Leave at least one solid tile between your tunnels and any water, and remember water can flow diagonally (Quickstart: Mining safety).

4. Labor assignment in the Work Details screen

Open the Labor menu with Y or the labor button at the bottom left. It has four tabs: Work details, Standing orders, Kitchen, and Stone use (Labor).

How work details work

Work details are on the left; picking one lists your citizens on the right with their relevant skill levels. Each work detail has one of three modes:

  • Everybody does this: every citizen may do these labors.
  • Only selected do this: only the dwarves you tick.
  • Nobody does this: nobody does it through this detail. Your ticks turn red and come back if you switch back to "Only selected".

The default work details are: Miners, Woodcutters, Hunters, Planters, Fisherdwarves, Plant gatherers, Stonecutters, Engravers, Haulers, Orderlies, Siege Operators. Mining, woodcutting, and hunting can only be assigned through their default details (Labor: Default work details).

Labors that aren't in any work detail are allowed for any unspecialized dwarf by default. That's why brewing, carpentry, and so on "just work" at the start. For more control, use Add new work detail to make your own (for example "Smiths", with furnace operating, weaponsmithing, armoring).

Specialization (the hammer-and-lock icon)

Each dwarf has a specialize toggle (red when on). A specialized dwarf only takes jobs from their assigned work details, workshops, or occupation. That's useful for keeping your best carpenter at the bench instead of hauling rocks.

Beginner setup

  1. Miners: Only selected. Tick your miner plus one or two more (each needs a pick).
  2. Woodcutters: your woodworker (needs an axe).
  3. Hunters and Fisherdwarves: Nobody does this for now. The quickstart advises this so dwarves aren't out alone where wildlife can kill them.
  4. Planters and Haulers: leave on Everybody. Later you may exclude miners and skilled crafters from hauling.
  5. Check the Kitchen tab: allow plump helmets for brewing and stop cooks from using them (and booze) as ingredients until you have plenty. New food types often start out with permissions you'll want to review.
Tool labors clash
Mining, woodcutting, and hunting each need a tool (pick, axe, crossbow). A dwarf can only have one of them; turning on a second silently turns off the first. Turn all three off for soldiers too, because the tool fights with their military uniform (Labor: Tool requirements).

Side note: veterans manage big forts with the fan tool Dwarf Therapist or with DFHack. You don't need either for a first fort.

5. Food, booze & water basics

Your current food and drink totals show in the status bar at the top of the screen. The Stocks screen shows details, and once you appoint a bookkeeper (see below) the counts become accurate.

Food

  • Plump helmets are the beginner's best friend. They grow underground in soil all year, can be eaten raw, and can be brewed. Brewing a plump helmet also gives you back seeds, which keeps the farm going.
  • Once the farm is running, add a second plot with variety. The quickstart suggests sweet pods in spring and summer, cave wheat or pig tails in autumn, and plump helmets in winter. Dwarves grumble about eating the same food over and over.
  • Kitchen (Build → Workshops → Farming → Kitchen): cooked meals stack ingredients together and keep well when stockpiled. Easy meals train your cook fastest.
  • Emergency food: gather wild plants from shrubs (Plant gatherers), or slaughter an animal at a Butcher's shop (mark it from the Pets/Livestock list).

Booze

Dwarves can survive on water, but going without alcohol makes them slower and unhappier. The quickstart says: build a still early. Each brew takes one plant and one empty barrel (or a stone rock pot from a craftsdwarf's workshop) and makes about 5 drinks. Set brewing to repeat once your crop comes in. A comfortable target is about ten drinks and ten meals per dwarf; much more than that is wasted effort early on.

Save wood
Make rock pots instead of wooden barrels, and stone tables and chairs instead of wooden ones. That saves wood for things that need it: beds and, later, charcoal.

Water

  • Early on, mark a Water Source zone along a river or pond so dwarves (and the injured) know where to drink. Drinking outdoors carries some risk from wildlife and weather.
  • Later, build a well (it needs a bucket, rope or chain, and a water source below it). Hospitals need water to clean wounds, and dwarves dislike "drinking water without a well" (Stress).
  • In cold climates surface water freezes in winter, and in very hot ones it can evaporate. That's one more reason to start somewhere temperate.

6. Defense basics

Even with Enemies off, wild animals and thieves can turn up. Plan for one controlled entrance that you can shut.

A door you can lock

The simplest defense is a door on your entry tunnel. Click a built door and set it to forbidden (locked). That keeps out dwarves, animals, and most invaders, but thieves can pick locks and building destroyers (like trolls) smash doors. Anything left on the door's tile props it open. Invaders who pass through a door "capture" it so you can't relock it until your own creature walks back through (Door). Doors also block trade wagons, so your depot needs to be on the outside of any door.

A drawbridge on a lever

  1. Build → Bridge across the entry tunnel, between the trade depot and your trap corridor. Set the bridge's raise direction. If you get it wrong, it retracts instead of standing up as a wall, and archers can shoot across. The arrow points toward the hinge (Quickstart: Drawbridge).
  2. Build a Lever somewhere safe inside, such as near the meeting area.
  3. Click the lever → Link lever → select the bridge. The quickstart says this takes 3 mechanisms in total; make them at a Mechanic's workshop.
  4. Test it once. Pulling the lever is a job a dwarf has to walk to.

Cage traps (and stone-fall traps)

  • A cage trap needs one mechanism and one cage (cages come from the carpenter's workshop or a metalsmith's forge). Build it from Build → Traps. A dwarf with the Mechanics labor installs it and reloads it (Trap: Cage trap).
  • Cage traps capture rather than kill. They also reveal ambushers, they're excellent against thieves and many invaders, and mechanism quality doesn't matter for them. Titans and forgotten beasts are immune.
  • A stone-fall trap needs only a mechanism and a stone, so it's an easy early option.
  • Your own conscious dwarves, pets, caravans, and guests don't trigger traps. Unconscious or sleeping dwarves can, though.
  • Traps block trade wagons. Leave a trap-free route to the depot or build traps where wagons won't need to go.
  • A 1×1 pasture with a dog near the entrance makes a cheap guard that spots thieves.

Your first militia squad

  1. Pick 5 or so dwarves without vital civilian jobs. Combat skills are a plus; the Play Now! fisherdwarf is a Proficient Fighter. Turn off their Mining, Woodcutting, and Hunting labors first.
  2. Open Nobles (N) and assign a Militia Commander.
  3. Open the squad sidebar (bottom right, or Q) → Create new squad → the Militia Commander's squad. Pick a default uniform and add members.
  4. Make a Barracks zone in a safe spot and assign the squad to it for training.
  5. In the squad list, set their schedule to Active/Training. Recruits report for training, though it may wait until the month changes.
  6. Give everyone at least a weapon and a helm. Sparring injuries are rare, but head injuries are the ones that end careers.

Source: Military quickstart, Squad. A squad holds up to 10 dwarves. Bonus: military training is one of the strongest happy-thought sources in the game (Stress).

Wealth attracts trouble
The quickstart warns that piling up wealth fast (gold crafts, heavy engraving) draws invaders and, past a certain point, titans. Build weapons, armor, and traps before luxuries.

7. Migrants, strange moods, stress & tantrums

Migrants

Expect your first migrant waves in your second and third seasons, typically 5–15 dwarves across the first two waves (Quickstart: Migrants). Before they arrive, have beds (or dormitory space), food, and booze ready. Look over each newcomer's skills, adjust their work details (they arrive with some already set), and turn spare peasants into haulers or recruits. Also appoint a Manager (unlocks work orders), a Bookkeeper (accurate stock counts), and a Broker (trading) in the Nobles screen. The manager and bookkeeper each need an office: a small room with a chair and table, zoned as an Office.

Strange moods

Once you have at least 20 dwarves, one may be struck by a strange mood. They grab a workshop and demand materials to build an artifact (Strange mood).

  • The workshop depends on their highest "moodable" skill: carpenter → Carpenter's workshop, mason or miner → Stoneworker's, mechanic → Mechanic's, and so on. No moodable skill means a Craftsdwarf's workshop. If the right workshop doesn't exist or can't be reached, build it.
  • Click the claimed workshop to see what they want (fey dwarves say it outright; secretive ones "sketch pictures"). They gather materials in order, so provide the next item on the list.
  • Success: an artifact, a big happy thought, and usually Legendary skill. Failure: after about a month and a half of waiting without what they need, they go insane and die soon after.
  • Fell and macabre moods only strike highly stressed dwarves. A fell dwarf murders someone for materials, which is one more reason to keep stress low.
  • Keep a few spare materials around: logs, stone, bars, leather, cloth, bones, gems.

Stress

Stress builds slowly from bad thoughts and drops slowly with good ones. If a dwarf stays stressed for a long time they become haggard, then harrowed, and a harrowed dwarf who has a traumatic experience can go insane (Stress). Check a dwarf's Thoughts and preferences tab to see why they're unhappy.

Tantrums & breakdowns

When short-term and long-term stress are both high, dwarves break down. Depending on personality, that's a tantrum (smashing things, picking fights), going oblivious, or depression. A tantrum can hurt others, which stresses them, and you get a tantrum spiral (Mental breakdown). The fix is prevention: beds, tables and chairs, booze variety, and keeping bodies cleaned up and buried. Build coffins and a tomb or graveyard early. A dwarf who keeps tantruming can be confined to a furnished room for a while, away from new stressors.

8. Common early mistakes & how fortresses die
  • Digging into water or an aquifer. Floods are fast and dwarves swim badly. Respect "damp" warnings, and don't channel or mine under a river until you know how.
  • Running out of booze or barrels. The still sits idle because there are no empty barrels or pots. Keep a few spare containers at all times.
  • Forgetting farm seasons. If you only set the current season, the plot sits fallow the rest of the year.
  • Nobody can do the job. Designations ignored? Check work details, tools (picks, axes), and whether anyone can actually reach the spot.
  • Multi-level pits and channels that dwarves fall into and can't climb out of.
  • Dwarves outside alone (hunters, fishers, wood haulers) running into wildlife. Hunting and fishing off early; check the units list's Others tab for dangerous animals.
  • General stockpile full of rocks and logs, so workshops clog with clutter. Use dedicated stone and wood piles and a 1×1 garbage dump zone for excess stone. Loose stone on the floor doesn't slow dwarves down, so you don't need to haul all of it.
  • Grazers starving with no pasture, or miasma from refuse piled inside.
  • Hauling everything: skilled dwarves spend their days carrying rocks. Exclude key crafters from hauling once you have migrants.
  • Getting rich too fast before you have any defense. See section 6.
  • Unhappiness spirals: no beds, no dining room, unburied dead, and a mood that fails. See section 7.
  • Trade depot unreachable. Caravans need a 3-wide path with no traps or stairs in the way. Trees and boulders can block it too.
  • Not keeping watch. Check announcements, the units screen, and your stocks regularly so you see trouble coming.
When it all goes wrong
Before you start over, look at the announcement log and figure out what happened. A dead fortress can also be reclaimed later in the same world (see the wiki's Reclaim fortress mode).

9. Glossary
Aquifer
A water-soaked soil or stone layer. Digging into it lets water in. Light ones can be managed; heavy ones are much harder.
Artifact
A unique, valuable item made by a dwarf in a strange mood.
Barracks
A zone where a squad trains and stores gear.
Channel
A dig order that removes a floor and leaves a ramp on the level below.
Embark
Choosing your site, dwarves, and supplies, then arriving with the wagon.
FPS
Game speed. Big maps, lots of trees, flowing water, and many dwarves slow it down.
Fun
Community slang for disaster ("Losing is fun!").
Labor / Work detail
A labor is permission to do a kind of job. A work detail is a named group of labors you assign to dwarves.
Megabeast / Titan / Forgotten beast
Huge, dangerous monsters. Immune to cage traps.
Miasma
Purple haze from rotting things. It stresses dwarves, so keep refuse outside.
Migrants
New dwarves who join your fort in waves.
Plump helmet
An underground mushroom. The classic first crop: edible raw and brewable into dwarven wine.
Savagery / Evil
Biome traits. Savage means monstrous wildlife; evil means undead, odd weather, and sometimes the dead rising.
Siege / Ambush
Organized attacks by hostile civilizations (goblins in vanilla).
Stockpile
A marked area where dwarves store a chosen category of items.
Strange mood
A sudden, all-consuming urge to make an artifact. Feed it materials or the dwarf goes insane.
Stress / Tantrum
Accumulated unhappiness, and the destructive breakdown it can cause.
Z-level
One layer of height or depth. Scroll the mouse wheel to move between them.
Zone
A marked area with a purpose: meeting area, bedroom, dining hall, pasture, water source, barracks, and so on.
